# CLAIMS — frozen claim set for completing the FHR3 manuscript

*How Many Hands Does It Take to Know Which Bot Is Better? Sample Complexity and Fair
Evaluation Across Poker Variants* (FullHouse Research #3)

This is the only source of quantitative claims for the draft. Every number traces to a
frozen output:

* core: `reports/final-analysis/`, pinned by its `MANIFEST.json`;
* RQ6: `reports/rq6/`, pinned by its `MANIFEST.json`, plus `reports/rq6/deep/` with its
  `SHA256SUMS`;
* RQ7: `reports/rq7/`, pinned by its `MANIFEST.json`, plus `reports/rq7/deep/` with its
  `SHA256SUMS`.

`paper/NUMBERS.md` (generated by `scripts/paper_numbers.py`) lists each value with its
source field. If a number here and in NUMBERS.md ever disagree, NUMBERS.md wins.

**Conventions (apply to every claim):**
* The unit of inference is the matched block. Effects are in BB/100 of candidate hands.
  "Required hands" are played hands (both sides of the block) for a two-sided α = 0.05
  test at 80% power and 1 BB/100, unless stated otherwise.
* Format medians are over pairings p1–p3. Nit p4 is excluded.
* "100BB formats" means NLHE 4-max, NLHE 6-max, Crazy Pineapple, PLO and Bomb Pot. Nit is
  excluded from 100BB-only spreads: it uses a different block protocol.
* Status: **H** = headline (abstract/intro/main text), **S** = secondary (main text, one or
  two sentences), **A** = appendix only.

## Ranked findings (by scientific importance)

### C1 [H]: Equal hand budgets are not equally informative
* **Wording:** "With matched blocks against a fixed reference population, the number of
  played hands needed to detect a 1 BB/100 difference with 80% power varies 61-fold across
  the seven formats (median over three candidate pairings), and 4.8-fold among the five
  100BB formats alone."
* **Values:** medians 0.34M (AoF 8BB), 4.26M (NLHE 4-max), 4.43M (NLHE 6-max),
  8.71M (Crazy Pineapple), 9.49M (Nit), 16.39M (PLO), 20.46M (Bomb Pot).
  Full-suite ratio 61×; 100BB ratio 4.8×.
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/ranking.json`, fields
  `metric.hands.formats[*].median`, `metric.hands.largest_over_smallest_median` and
  `metric.hands.largest_over_smallest_median_100bb_excl_nit`.
* **Uncertainty:** each median has a bootstrap CI in the same file (`median_ci`,
  sampling-only). Format medians are precise to a few percent. Pairing choice, not
  sampling, is the dominant uncertainty (C7).
* **Caveat:** the numbers depend on the benchmark agents, the reference population and a
  fixed 1 BB/100 target. A 1 BB/100 edge is not equally meaningful across formats. The 61×
  relies on AoF's bounded 8BB stacks. This is **not** a difficulty ranking of poker
  variants.
* **Figures:** F1, F2. **Section:** "How many hands?"

### C2 [H]: Uncertainty is concentrated in rare, decisive big pots
* **Wording:** "In every 100BB format, matched blocks containing a pot of at least one
  starting stack carry 79–97% of the total squared deviation of the block difference D. The
  blocks that are both big-pot and decisive (D ≠ 0) are only 0.6–22% of all blocks."
* **Values:**
  * big-pot share of squared deviation: 79–97% (NLHE4 79–81%, NLHE6 83–85%, CP 88–89%,
    PLO 93–94%, Bomb 96–97%);
  * big-pot-and-decisive blocks: 0.6–21.6% of all blocks;
  * top 1% of blocks: 33–95% of squared deviation. A Gaussian sample would give ≈ 8.5%
    (analytic reference in `paper/figures/data/F3_variance_concentration.csv`).
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/allin.csv` (`share_pot_ge_1stack`,
  `frac_blocks_big_decisive`) and `reports/final-analysis/core/pilot_table.csv`
  (`conc_top0.01`).
* **Proxy note:** blocks with an all-in carry 81–97% (`share_allin`). All-in status is a
  proxy. In NL it slightly overcounts, because uncontested shoves have small pots. In PLO
  it undercounts: pot-limit betting builds big pots without all-ins. In PLO, blocks with a
  big pot but no all-in are 6.3–6.6% of blocks and carry 12–13% of the squared deviation;
  the big-pot share is 93–94% against an all-in share of 82–83%.
* **Caveat:** the "0.6–22%" is a share of **all** blocks, not of decisive blocks. The
  frequency/magnitude decomposition (C2b) is exploratory.
* **Figures:** F3, F4a. **Section:** "Where does the uncertainty come from?"

### C2b [S]: Frequency vs magnitude (exploratory)
* **Wording:** "Within a format, how often big pots occur is set by the format and the
  reference population, and pairings differ mainly in how much a big pot moves D. Across
  100BB formats, differences in big-pot frequency account for most of the cross-format
  spread in block variance."
* **Values:**
  * within NLHE6, the all-in block frequency is 0.078 / 0.078 / 0.083 for p1–p3;
  * across the 15 100BB pairings, a log-variance decomposition gives ≈ 73% frequency and
    32% magnitude (big-pot definition). The terms can exceed 100% in total because the
    covariance terms are split evenly.
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/allin.csv` (`frac_blocks_allin`) and
  `reports/final-analysis/allin_regression.json` (`log_var_decomposition_100bb_big_pot`).
* **Caveat:** descriptive, not causal; 15 pairings.
* **Figure:** F4a.

### C3 [H]: Stack depth changes both the amount and the kind of variance
* **Wording, amount:** "With identical cards and the same stack-unaware agents, 100BB
  stacks need about 7× the matched blocks of 20BB stacks and about 2× those of 50BB stacks,
  for each of three pairings."
  * Values: 7.1× / 7.2× / 6.8× vs 20BB (paired bootstrap CIs within 5.7–8.6×);
    2.1× / 2.1× / 2.0× vs 50BB (CIs within 1.7–2.4×).
  * Mechanism (p2, 100 → 50 → 20BB): all-in block frequency 0.08 → 0.16 → 0.29, while
    squared deviation per all-in block falls 1,869 → 535 → 87.
  * Source: `reports/final-analysis/stack.json` (`sample_ratio_vs_100_common`, inverted;
    `sample_ratio_ci`) and `reports/final-analysis/allin.csv` (`frac_blocks_allin`,
    `mean_dev2_allin`).
* **Wording, kind (from RQ6):** "Exact all-in EV removes a median 12% of the block variance
  at 100BB, 28% at 20BB and 90% in 8BB All-in-or-Fold. Shallow-stack uncertainty is
  increasingly removable runout luck; deep-stack uncertainty largely is not."
  * Values: median variance ratios 0.878 (100BB), 0.716 (20BB), 0.098 (AoF). The 100BB −
    20BB difference in median ratio is 0.16 [0.10, 0.23].
  * Source: `reports/rq6/rq6_summary.csv` (`var_ratio_vs_raw`) and
    `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_deep.json` (`allin_ev_dose_response`).
* **Caveat:** agents are not stack-aware. AoF differs from NLHE in format as well as stack
  (4-max, fold-or-shove only).
* **Figures:** F4b, F5. **Sections:** "Where does the uncertainty come from?" and "How much
  can be removed?"

### C4 [H]: Naively monitoring an ordinary confidence interval fails badly on these streams
* **Wording:** "If an evaluator re-checks an ordinary 95% interval after every block (from
  block 200 onward) and stops when it excludes zero, 58–64% of null evaluation streams
  produce a false winner within 250,000 blocks. When a real edge exists, 8.5% of stops name
  the wrong agent."
* **Values:**
  * null false exclusion: heavy NLHE6 58.0% [55.8, 60.1], CP 62.8%, PLO 60.6%,
    Normal 63.9%, t3 63.4% (2,000 reps each);
  * with burn-in 5,000 blocks, still 44.2–44.8%;
  * heavy stream timing: 16% of runs falsely stopped by 1k blocks, 36% by 10k, 52% by 100k,
    58% by 250k; median first false stop 3,908 blocks (1,964–3,908 across distributions);
  * wrong-sign stops, non-null: 854 / 9,995 = 8.5%, up to 12.6% in the weak-effect cells;
  * real orderings: across 1,000 random orderings of the same 250k NLHE6 p1 blocks, the
    true (corpus) mean is excluded in 44.8% [41.7, 47.9], and the wrong direction is
    declared in 11.2%.
* **Source:**
  * `reports/rq7/coverage.csv` (method = peek) and `reports/rq7/min_n_sensitivity.csv`;
  * `reports/rq7/deep/null_timing.csv` and `reports/rq7/deep/wrong_sign.json`;
  * `reports/rq7/stopping.csv` and `reports/rq7/real_permutations.csv`.
* **Caveat:** the Type-I inflation itself is textbook. The contribution is its size, how
  early it happens, and the wrong-sign rate on realistic block streams and budgets.
* **Figure:** F6a. **Section:** "How should evaluation stop?"

### C5 [H]: An asymptotic confidence sequence restores empirical time-uniform validity at a predictable cost
* **Wording:** "An asymptotic confidence sequence missed the true mean at any time up to
  250,000 blocks in only 2.4–3.4% of null streams, below the nominal 5% on every tested
  distribution, including the heaviest poker stream (2.4%). It never stopped in the wrong
  direction (0 of 9,768 decisions). To match the decision rate of a precommitted fixed-N
  test it needs about 1.6–1.7× that test's sample."
* **Values:**
  * miscoverage: heavy 2.40% [1.8, 3.2], CP 2.70%, PLO 2.55%, t3 3.20%,
    Normal 3.40% [2.7, 4.3];
  * no null miss before 8,739 blocks; results identical for burn-in 200 / 1,000 / 5,000;
  * wrong sign 0 / 9,768 (one-sided 95% upper bound 0.031%);
  * power-matched cost: 1.69–1.73 × N80 (N80 = 25k) and 1.60–1.63 × N80 (N80 = 100k);
    median stop 0.87–1.13 × N80;
  * width relative to the t-interval: 4.63× at 1k blocks, 1.97× at 10k, 1.67× at 25k,
    1.55× at 100k (= t*), 1.57× at 250k;
  * real orderings: 0.9% [0.5, 1.7] miscoverage;
  * fixed-N single look at 100k: 4.45–4.95% (nominal).
* **Source:**
  * `reports/rq7/coverage.csv` (method = asymp, fixed) and
    `reports/rq7/min_n_sensitivity.csv`;
  * `reports/rq7/deep/null_timing.csv` (`earliest_miss`) and
    `reports/rq7/deep/wrong_sign.json`;
  * `reports/rq7/deep/stopping_cost.csv` (`power_matched_ratio`,
    `cs_median_over_fixed_n`) and `reports/rq7/deep/width_inflation.csv`;
  * `reports/rq7/real_permutations.csv`.
* **Caveat:** coverage is **asymptotic and empirical**: t* = 100k, horizon 250k, five
  distributions. Not a finite-sample guarantee. Permutation coverage is conservative by
  construction (sampling without replacement).
* **Figures:** F6a, F6b. **Section:** "How should evaluation stop?"

### C6 [H/S]: At deep stacks, chance correction removes a bounded share, and the tail survives
* **Wording:** "At 100BB, all-in EV, fitted MIVAT and fitted AIVAT leave 88%, 62% and 58%
  of the raw block variance (sample-efficiency gains 1.14×, 1.62×, 1.72×). Correcting chance
  beyond the final all-in runout does most of the work; correcting known agent actions adds
  little."
* **Values:**
  * NLHE 100BB variance ratios:
    * all-in EV: 0.878 [p1 0.878 (0.812–0.938); p2 0.857 (0.809–0.897); p3 0.924 (0.874–0.979)];
    * MIVAT: 0.616 [0.596–0.663 across pairings];
    * AIVAT: 0.580 [0.571–0.590]. All CIs exclude 1.
  * Paired increments: MIVAT / all-in EV = 0.70–0.72, all CIs < 1. AIVAT / MIVAT = 0.93
    [0.84, 1.03], 0.97 [0.91, 1.04], 0.89 [0.83, 0.95].
  * Hands saved at 1 BB/100: 12.2% / 38.4% / 42.0% (medians).
* **Tail:**
  * big-pot blocks still carry 82–87% of the corrected squared deviation;
  * p1 excess kurtosis 183 (raw) → 177 / 229 / 265;
  * p1 top-1% share 0.94 → 0.93 / 0.95 / 0.94.
* **Source:** `reports/rq6/rq6_summary.csv`; `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_pairwise.csv`;
  `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_tails.csv`.
* **Required interpretation wording:** the variance that remains reflects "strategic
  divergence plus chance variation not captured by the fitted value model". Do **not** call
  it "strategic variance".
* **Caveat:** the value function is linear, with 7 features, fitted on 36,000 training
  hands (separate seed). A richer model could remove more.
* **Figure:** F5. **Section:** "How much can be removed?"

### C6b [H, illustrative]: The same data can go from unresolved to resolved
* **Wording:** "In 8BB All-in-or-Fold, replacing realized all-in outcomes by their exact
  expectation turns the unresolved production-policy vs 10%-blend comparison
  (+0.27 BB/100, 95% CI [−0.07, +0.62]) into a decisive one (+0.41 [+0.30, +0.52]) on the
  same 50,000 blocks. Raw evaluation would need about 511,000 blocks for that precision."
* **Values:**
  * raw: z = 1.55; all-in EV: z = 7.38; AoF median variance ratio 0.098 (≈ 10.2× sample
    efficiency; per pairing 7.7–10.7×);
  * the estimator-minus-raw difference for p1 has z = +0.79, so the change in the point
    estimate is within sampling noise.
* **Source:** `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_deep.json`
  (`settings['aof4-8bb'].pairings['p1-prodv2-vs-blend10'].precision`) and
  `reports/rq6/rq6_summary.csv` (`mean_diff_z`).
* **Caveat:** report both point estimates. The estimand is unchanged; the two are unbiased
  estimates of the same quantity that differ on a finite sample. Do not frame +0.41 as a
  "correction" of +0.27.
* **Figure:** F5 inset.

### C7 [S]: Candidate/reference pairing is a first-order design choice
* **Wording:** "Within each 100BB format, the three candidate pairings change the required
  hands by 2.8–8.5×. That is comparable to the 4.8× spread across 100BB formats."
* **Values:** max/min over p1–p3 is 2.8× (CP) to 8.5× (Bomb); across all seven formats
  2.7–10.0×.
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/ranking.json` (`within_format_spread_100bb_excl_nit`,
  `formats[*].pairing_spread`).
* **Caveat:** three pairings per format. The perturbations are not identical across formats
  (AoF and Bomb p1 are format-specific). Same-role comparisons across formats are
  descriptive only. The claim is scoped to the 100BB formats.
* **Figure:** F2.

### C8 [S/A]: Standard fixed-N matched-block power calculations remain adequate
* **Wording:** "Despite zero inflation and excess kurtosis up to about 300, the normal-theory
  sample size delivered 78–82% empirical power (target 80%) and 3–6% null rejection at
  500–10,000 blocks, and 4.5–5.0% null rejection at a single precommitted look at 100,000
  blocks."
* **Values:** power 0.779–0.815; null rejection 0.028–0.057; MC SE 0.006.
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/d_collapse.csv` and `reports/rq7/coverage.csv`
  (method = fixed).
* **Caveat:** resampling cannot produce tails that were never observed.
* **Placement:** appendix, plus one main-text sentence.

### C9 [S, guidance]: Kurtosis predicts how large a pilot must be
* **Wording:** "A 10,000-block pilot estimated the final required sample size within about
  30% (0.86–1.28× the final value). The relative error of the pilot σ follows the
  delta-method rule √((κ+2)/4n), which gives n ≈ 384(κ+2) pilot blocks for ±5% precision in
  σ at 95% confidence."
* **Values:**
  * prefix ratio 0.86–1.28×; disjoint 10k windows 0.75–1.33×; 97% of windows within ±20%;
  * observed/predicted window-SD median 1.00.
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/convergence.csv`.
* **Caveat:** κ must itself be estimated from the pilot. The earlier "±7%" claim is
  **withdrawn**.
* **Placement:** guidance paragraph; derivation in the appendix.

### C10 [S]: NLHE 4-max and 6-max cost about the same per played hand
* **Wording:** "Per played hand, 4-max and 6-max NLHE are statistically indistinguishable
  (6-max / 4-max required hands 1.04 [0.98, 1.11]). 4-max needs about 1.4× more matched
  blocks only because its blocks contain fewer hands."
* **Values:** hands ratio 1.04 [0.98, 1.11]; blocks ratio 1.44 [1.35, 1.53].
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/ranking.json`, field `metric.{hands,blocks}.pairs`.
* **Caveat:** this is not a player-count effect claim. The CI is sampling-only, with
  pairings held fixed.

### C11 [S]: Block construction changes the statistical appearance, not the mechanism (Nit Game)
* **Wording:** "In the Nit Game, the large win-rate gaps (+30.3, +17.3, −10.1 BB/100) are
  almost entirely nit-penalty transfers. The chip-play components are −1.0, −0.2 and
  −1.5 BB/100. Blocks look near-normal (excess kurtosis 4–12) only because each averages
  about 95 hands; big-pot blocks still carry 85–87% of the squared deviation (p1–p3)."
* **Source:** `reports/final-analysis/nit.json` and `reports/final-analysis/allin.csv`.
* **Caveat:** do not present Nit gaps as poker skill. Nit p4 is a mechanism check only.
* **Placement:** one paragraph in the mechanism section; table in the appendix.

### C12 [S]: Variance reduction is cheap to buy only in some settings
* **Wording:** "In our simulator, all-in EV costs 1.0–1.1× raw simulation time in NLHE and
  1.7× in AoF; MIVAT costs 1.4× and AIVAT 1.9×. At 100BB, AIVAT's 1.72× sample-efficiency
  gain is therefore a slight net wall-clock loss (0.91–0.94×), while all-in EV in AoF is a
  4.5–6.3× net gain."
* **Source:** `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_deep.json` (`estimator_bench`) and
  `reports/rq6/deep/rq6_efficiency.csv`.
* **Caveat:** hardware- and implementation-specific. When hands are expensive (live or
  human play), sample efficiency is what matters.
* **Placement:** one main-text sentence; table in the appendix.

### C13 [A]: Estimator correctness
* max |z(mean(estimator − raw))| = 1.29 over all RQ6 rows (gate: 3);
* raw D reproduces the frozen corpora block for block (compare: paired, corr 1.000);
* self-play is exactly 0.

Source: `reports/rq6/rq6_summary.csv` and `reports/rq6/rq6_summary.json`. This is a
runtime check; unbiasedness rests on construction plus the exact toy-game and PokerKit
enumeration tests.

### C14 [S]: The real-order example (NLHE 6-max p2)
* **Wording:** "Read in its actual block order, the flagship 6-max comparison looks
  decisively negative after 204 blocks (−27.9 BB/100, naive 95% CI [−54.0, −1.8]). The full
  250,000 blocks show a small positive edge (+1.28 [+0.43, +2.13], z = 2.94). The confidence
  sequence never excludes zero and ends at [−0.06, +2.61]."
* **Also:** across 28 real corpus streams, naive peeking stopped in 23. Four of those stops
  contradict the full-corpus sign; the confidence sequence stopped in 10 and never
  contradicted it.
* **Source:** `reports/rq7/real_orderings.csv`,
  `reports/rq7/deep/trajectory_nlhe6_p2.csv` and
  `reports/rq7/deep/real_orderings_sign.csv`.
* **Figure:** F6b.
* **Correction:** use −27.9 [−54.0, −1.8] (exact at block 204). Earlier notes quoted
  −27.8 [−53.7, −1.8], which is the grid point at block 205.
